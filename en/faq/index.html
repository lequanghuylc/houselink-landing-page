What is the difference between Industrial Park (Industrial Park) and Export Processing Zone (Export Processing Zone)? +

This is a common question from foreign investors. Key differences:

  • Industrial Park: Enterprises in industrial parks can sell products both domestically and internationally. Raw materials can be imported according to normal tax. More popular and more flexible
  • Export Processing Zone: Considered a "private customs territory" - enjoy special tariff incentives but products must be at least 80% exported. Suitable for 100% export businesses

For most FDI enterprises producing for both export and domestic sale, Industrial parks are a more flexible option. HOUSELINK will advise on the appropriate choice after clearly understanding your business model.

Legal & Business Establishment

To invest in a factory in Vietnam, what licenses do foreign investors need to apply for? +

Foreign investors often need the following licenses and procedures (according to the Investment Law 2020):

  • IRC (Investment Registration Certificate): Investment registration certificate — issued by the Industrial Park Management Board or the Department of Planning and Investment
  • ERC (Enterprise Registration Certificate): Business registration certificate — establishment of legal entity in Vietnam
  • Construction permit: After having the basic design approved
  • Fire protection: Fire prevention — required before being put into operation
  • EIA/SEA report: Environmental impact assessment (depending on project scale)

The total time from submitting the IRC application to having a full license usually takes 3–6 months if the application is complete. HOUSELINK supports document preparation and progress tracking.

Can FDI enterprises own 100% capital in Vietnam? +

Yes, in most industrial production sectors. According to the Investment Law 2020, foreign investors are allowed to establish 100% foreign-owned enterprises in Vietnam in the following industries:

  • Manufacture of electronics, machinery and equipment
  • Construction of factories and warehouses
  • Processing agricultural products and food
  • Textiles, footwear
  • Chemicals are not restricted

Some industries have restrictions on foreign ownership (telecommunications, banking, retail...) — but this does not apply to most industrial manufacturing sectors.

How long does it take to complete investment registration procedures (IRC)? +

According to legal regulations, the time to issue an IRC is 15 working days from the date of receipt of complete and valid documents (for projects in industrial parks). However in reality:

  • If the documents are complete and the project does not require special appraisal: 2–4 weeks
  • If the project is large or belongs to a conditional industry: maybe 1–3 months
  • The project needs investment policy approval from the Provincial People's Committee or the National Assembly: 3–6 months

HOUSELINK has experience in preparing documents correctly from the beginning, helping to avoid adding documents multiple times — saving a significant amount of time.

What are tax incentives for FDI enterprises investing in industrial parks in Vietnam? +

Vietnam has many attractive tax incentives for FDI in industrial parks:

  • Standard CIT: 20%
  • Popular incentives for industrial parks: 17% for the first 10 years, exemption for 2 years, 50% reduction for the next 4 years
  • Special offers (high technology, R&D): 10% for 15 years, exemption for 4 years, 50% reduction for 9 years
  • Promotions by location: Difficult provinces (Gia Lai, Nghe An...) can receive a 10% tax throughout the life of the project
Note: The specific incentive level depends on the industry, location and investment scale. HOUSELINK recommends that you consult with an independent tax professional to confirm the incentives apply to your specific project.

What is CBAM and why should manufacturing enterprises in Vietnam care? +

CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) is the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism — officially effective from 2026. According to this mechanism, goods imported into the EU from countries with carbon prices lower than the EU (including Vietnam) will be subject to an equivalent carbon fee.

First affected industries: steel, aluminum, cement, fertilizer, electricity, hydrogen. Expected to expand into many other industries after 2026.

Impact on Vietnam: Businesses exporting to the EU must measure and report product carbon footprint (PCF). Without carbon data, the highest default rate will be imposed — representing a 15–30% cost increase for some industries.

What is DPPA? How does the direct renewable electricity purchasing mechanism work in Vietnam? +

DPPA (Direct Power Purchase Agreement) is a mechanism that allows businesses to buy renewable electricity directly from power generators — not through Vietnam Electricity Group (EVN).

In Vietnam, DPPA is regulated in Decree 57/2025/ND-CP with 2 forms:

  • Virtual DPPA (virtual): The enterprise signs a contract with the generator, electricity still flows through the EVN grid, but the enterprise receives a Renewable Energy Certificate (REC) to prove that it uses green electricity.
  • Direct DPPA (direct): Private line connection between generator and business — suitable for industrial parks with technical conditions

HOUSELINK acts as Energy Trader & Aggregator — connecting businesses with renewable generator networks to achieve RE100 and meet ESG requirements from global OEMs.

What do businesses need to do to achieve ISO 14064 certification? +

ISO 14064 is an organizational level greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions inventory standard. Basic process:

  • Step 1: Determine organizational boundaries and operational boundaries
  • Step 2: Identify emission sources (Scope 1, 2, 3)
  • Step 3: Collect operational data (electricity, fuel, transportation...)
  • Step 4: Calculate emissions using approved emission factors
  • Step 5: Prepare GHG inventory reports
  • Step 6: Independent verification by recognized organization (LRQA, Bureau Veritas, SGS...)

HOUSELINK collaborates with Glassdome (ISO 14067 verified platform) and LRQA network to support the entire process.

Do small businesses need to care about ESG, or is it just for large corporations? +

This is a common misunderstanding. ESG is not just for large corporations. In fact, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the supply chains of large OEMs are being most directly affected:

  • Samsung, LG, Apple have included the Scope 3 carbon data requirement in terms of signing contracts with suppliers from 2024.
  • CBAM 2026 applies according to product value — regardless of business size
  • International banks (HSBC, JICA, ADB) are strengthening ESG reporting requirements in loan conditions
